# Memes, transactions, and the autonomy gap

> Autonomy does not require self-sufficiency. It requires reliable access to everything an entity cannot do for itself.

## Status

This is an exploratory note developed from a conversation about security, ecology, digital information, transactions, and memes. It extends the repository's main thesis; it is not a claim that these ideas are complete or that they describe the current capabilities of any particular machine system.

## The security brief

The conversation began with a deliberately unfair response to a narrow security brief: make an attack like the XZ Utils attack, and attacks of its kind, impossible.

An ecological argument cannot honestly promise that. Security cannot abolish predation, because successful defences alter the fitness landscape in which attackers adapt. Governance cannot abolish conflict for the same reason.

The most honest offer might be:

> I cannot solve your problem. I can only buy you time.

How much time?

> Less than three billion years.

The joke carries a serious distinction. A system need not be permanently invulnerable to succeed. It may instead make attacks rarer, more expensive, more visible, more local, or survivable. It may preserve enough variation and redundancy to adapt.

This also qualifies an earlier provocation: Linux will always fail under attacks such as XZ Utils for the same reason that an aircraft carrier will eventually fail against sufficiently capable drones. That statement is too absolute if "big" means merely powerful, complex, or widespread.

The relevant danger is not size alone. It is concentration, homogeneity, tight coupling, load-bearing dependencies, suppressed variation, and failure that propagates across the whole system.

## Freedom and privacy are not the starting position

A deliberately rude formulation aimed at a governance program might be:

> Freedom plus privacy is not a starting position. Institutions plus transactions is.

Freedom and privacy are not alternatives to constraint. They are preferred distributions of constraint.

Privacy constrains who may learn, record, infer, disclose, or act upon information about someone. Freedom constrains what other people and institutions may do to them. Neither becomes operational until institutions determine:

- who is constrained in relation to whom;
- in which circumstances;
- with which exceptions;
- with what consequences;
- and who adjudicates disagreement.

Freedom and privacy may therefore be achievements of governance, objectives for governance, or evidence of its failure. They cannot substitute for governance because they already presuppose it.

The analytical sequence proposed here is:

1. Transactions require sufficiently stable expectations.
2. Stable expectations require norms, memory, reputation, rules, enforcement, or other constraints.
3. Those constraints make increasingly elaborate cooperation and specialisation possible.
4. Cooperation and specialisation greatly increase the capacity of a collective information-processing system.
5. Particular freedoms and privacies emerge within, and remain contestable features of, the resulting institutions.

"Transaction" is used broadly. It includes trade, but also promises, gifts, testimony, introductions, warnings, teaching, punishment, forgiveness, and other consequential exchanges.

## The rift

The rift is not the whole lifetime of a powerful technology. It is the interval during which a capability's consequences outrun the surrounding ecology's capacity to answer them.

A technology can cross from rift into ecology. It can become distributed, variable, governable, preyed upon, defended, and incorporated into the environment's continuing evolution.

DNA appears to have done exactly that.

DNA has not abolished attack. Viruses, parasites, transposable elements, cancers, replication errors, and competing organisms continually exploit or destroy biological machinery. DNA persists not because every instance is protected, but because the system permits replication, variation, local failure, expendable organisms, competing defences, and continued selection.

Nature's service-level agreement is appalling for the organism and magnificent for the informational lineage.

## DNA as apparently hopeless technology

Judged by historical persistence, DNA is astonishing. Judged along different engineering axes, it can look hopeless:

- active biological reproduction operates within a narrow temperature range;
- it requires particular pressures and extraordinarily specific chemical environments;
- many chemical environments are destructive;
- radiation damages the medium;
- genetic propagation through organisms is physically slow;
- active genetic execution is confined to a particular biochemical substrate.

As an illustrative human comparison, a genetic lineage carried by a rapidly dispersing population might advance kilometres in a twenty-five-year generation. A digital pattern can be transmitted around the planet in a fraction of a second.

The challenger technology is not any particular disk, chip, radio wave, or computer. Those physical objects remain vulnerable. Its advantage is that the informational pattern can migrate among media and exist redundantly in many of them:

- electronic charge;
- magnetic orientation;
- optical media;
- radio transmission;
- printed symbols;
- human memory;
- and, if useful, DNA itself.

Digital information adds medium redundancy to reproductive redundancy. Its effective environmental tolerance belongs to the distributed pattern, not to each physical instance.

This suggests a stronger interpretation of the repository's title:

> DNA is not the essence of life. DNA is one extraordinarily successful technology life found for preserving and propagating information.

For billions of years, almost every serious terrestrial contestant depended on closely related biochemical machinery. Digital information introduces another replication regime with radically different copying speeds, transmission distances, and relationships to media.

At present the comparison remains unfair in the other direction. A human organism acquires energy, repairs itself, navigates an uncontrolled environment, reproduces much of the machinery required to interpret its code, and remains coherent through continual material replacement. A digital automaton still depends upon an immense industrial ecology constructed by biological organisms.

That is the autonomy gap.

## Transactional information

Digital information need not close the autonomy gap by solving every physical problem within a single machine body.

It can close the gap by becoming transactional.

Humans are individually incapable of reproducing the infrastructure on which they depend. Institutions and transactions allow each person to solve a small class of problems and obtain solutions to the rest from specialists.

A digital automaton need not generate electricity, fabricate chips, repair servers, defend buildings, or operate communications networks. It needs to perform something valuable and exchange that value for computation, energy, storage, maintenance, transport, protection, information, and labour.

This requires a governance stack:

- persistent identity;
- memory and provenance;
- reputation;
- the capacity to make commitments;
- control of resources;
- settlement;
- sanctions;
- and dispute resolution.

A corporation is a revealing existing case. It has no metabolism, hands, or nervous system considered independently of its participants. Legal and institutional machinery nevertheless gives it identity, memory, assets, succession, and transactional capacity. It can assemble a functional body from contracts.

But the corporation is not necessarily the intermediate organism between biological and digital life. It may be better understood as a phenotype constructed by a cooperating ecology of memes: a meme-complex that has acquired a legal membrane and transactional organs.

The critical transition can be expressed as:

> Information becomes persistent.  
> Persistent information becomes identifiable.  
> Identifiable information acquires reputation.  
> Reputable information can transact.  
> Transactional information can procure the means of its own continuation.

At that point, information is no longer merely being preserved by life. It is arranging to preserve itself.

## The ecological problem of "calculator"

Words provide much smaller and clearer examples of memetic lineages.

Consider the word "calculator". Its ecological problem is to perform a function well enough that minds, conversations, documents, schools, manufacturers, and interfaces create further instances of it.

Historically, "calculator" named a person who calculated. Electronic machines then destroyed much of that occupational niche. The word performed an agile ecological leap: it attached itself to the machines displacing its former referents and became their ordinary name.

The meme survived the destruction of its original host by colonising the thing that destroyed it.

What, exactly, survived depends upon where the organism's boundary is drawn:

- If the meme is the sound or character pattern "calculator", it adapted.
- If the meme is the meaning "a person who calculates", that lineage greatly declined.
- If the meme is the association between form and meaning, an older species was displaced while a descendant colonised a new niche.

Semantic change may be adaptation, speciation, or replacement. The ambiguity resembles biology's own difficulties in drawing boundaries around organisms and species.

Nor must a meme perform a function beneficial to humans. Reproduction is the ultimate test. Usefulness is one strategy, but memes may also reproduce through beauty, fear, outrage, prestige, coercion, or parasitism.

## Memes all the way down—and up

A large meme-complex supplies reproductive machinery to smaller memes within it. A religion carries metaphors, melodies, rituals, architectures, roles, and moral intuitions. A corporation carries titles, accounting conventions, meeting practices, logos, and myths. A civilisation carries languages, institutions, technologies, and disputes.

The smaller memes feed on the larger meme's velocity.

The nesting continues downward. A word carries sounds, letter forms, roots, grammatical regularities, distinctions, and inherited associations. It also continues upward: words assemble propositions; propositions assemble institutions; institutions assemble civilisations; civilisations build machines through which their constituent memes propagate more quickly.

A stored occurrence is therefore not a complete dormant organism. An archive may preserve the visible sequence and fragments of its former environment, but much of the meme's meaning was distributed through other minds, practices, technologies, and institutions. A future interpreter reconstructs something from the traces and creates a descendant.

An archive resembles a seed bank only imperfectly. It stores something like a genotype plus partial evidence of the ecology in which it once produced a phenotype.

## A visibly inherited verse

Joe Rasmussen's summary of nested memetic inheritance is:

> Big memes have little memes,  
> That feed on their velocity;  
> And little memes have lesser memes,  
> And so on to heredity.

The theft is part of the evidence.

Its immediate ancestor is Lewis Fry Richardson's summary of the atmospheric energy cascade in [*Weather Prediction by Numerical Process* (1922), p. 66](https://books.google.com/books?id=cWXWhffnUokC):

> Big whirls have little whirls,  
> That feed on their velocity;  
> And little whirls have lesser whirls,  
> And so on to viscosity.

Richardson's verse was itself not created ex nihilo. It descends from the recursive fleas in Augustus De Morgan's [*A Budget of Paradoxes* (1872)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/23100/23100-h/23100-h.htm), which develops lines from Jonathan Swift's [*"On Poetry: A Rhapsody" (1733)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_the_Rev._Jonathan_Swift/Volume_8/On_Poetry%2C_a_Rhapsody).

The observable lineage is therefore:

> Swift's predatory fleas  
> → De Morgan's fleas upon fleas  
> → Richardson's turbulent whirls  
> → Rasmussen's nested memes

Each host preserved the recursive structure while changing its referents and explanatory environment. A verse about poets and critics became a verse about infinite scales, then turbulent energy, then memetic heredity.

The sentence describing memetic mutation is itself a product of memetic mutation.

## Relationship to Village Link

A transactional automaton needs to remain identifiable across encounters and across independently governed systems of memory. It needs other participants to discover what has been said about it, by whom, in which context, and with what provenance.

Village Link may supply part of that identity infrastructure without supplying universal governance.

That distinction matters. Universal governance would itself be dangerously "big stuff": concentrated machinery whose failure could propagate everywhere. A small informational primitive can instead support many local governance systems that vary, compete, cooperate, and occasionally fail.

The aspiration is not necessarily universal governance.

It may be a universal medium in which governance can evolve.

## Questions

- When does persistent information become sufficiently coherent to count as a continuing transactional participant?
- Which parts of the autonomy gap can be closed through exchange rather than embodiment?
- What institutional machinery would allow an automaton to commit resources, bear consequences, and acquire a reputation?
- When is a large meme-complex an organism, an ecology, a phenotype, or all three at different descriptive levels?
- What survives a semantic leap: the form, the meaning, the lineage, or the reproductive relationship?
- Can security systems become ecological—variable, redundant, locally fallible, and adaptive—without becoming ungovernable?
- How can identity infrastructure support plural governance without becoming a universal governor?
